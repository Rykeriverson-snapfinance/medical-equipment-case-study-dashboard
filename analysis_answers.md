# Case Study Answers

## Program Funnel

The program shows meaningful demand across 2,263 prequalification applications, 85 merchants, and five regions. Prequalification approval is high at 85.7%, and final approval is strong once applicants submit the full application at 92.7% of full applications.

The main funnel leak is continuation. Only 39.8% of prequalified applicants submit a full application. End-to-end completion is 25.6%, so management should focus on moving more prequalified applicants through the full application rather than only changing final approval criteria.

## Special Underwriting And Swap-In Logic

Swap-in applications represent a smaller share of volume, but they convert better end-to-end than non-swap applications. This supports the argument that the special logic is preserving incremental volume from stores that still prefer Snap.

The tradeoff is risk. Swap-in applicants have higher average prequalification and final risk scores than non-swap applicants, and 83.1% of swap-in applications appear to rely on the $1,500 approval floor.

## Risk Profile

The risk migration story is directionally positive for applicants who continue. Among full applications with final grades, 59.1% improved, 33.3% stayed the same, and 7.6% worsened. Average risk score improved from 0.196 at prequalification to 0.182 at final application.

The largest caution area is prequalification grade F. Grade F has the most application volume but lower end-to-end completion, heavy reliance on the $1,500 floor, and higher risk.

## Ticket Size And Estimated Funded Volume

The dataset does not contain actual funded amount. The dashboard uses final approval amount for completed applications as an estimated funded amount proxy. That is useful for directional comparison, but it should not be presented as actual funded volume unless Snap confirms that final approval amount equals funded amount.

## Repayment And Profitability

The repayment extract adds 520 account-level repayment rows that de-duplicate to 517 unique matched applications, or about 89.3% of completed applications. Across the matched repayment records, projected amount paid exceeds net funded amount by roughly $215.7K, with a projected payback multiple of 1.24x.

The repayment view still argues for guardrails. Missed payment at day 45 is 16.8%, no payments by day 60 is 3.9%, 30-plus past due by day 120 is 7.4%, and charge-off status is 8.7%. Early payoff by day 120 is 28.8%, which helps account performance but should be interpreted with product economics and timing.

Profitability remains partially answered rather than fully proven. The dashboard uses projected amount paid minus net funded amount as a profit proxy, but the file does not include cost of capital, servicing cost, realized recovery timing, charge-off amount, or a standard-policy counterfactual.

## Recommendation

Continue the program selectively. Expand or prioritize lower-risk merchants and regions with strong completion, meaningful funded volume, and acceptable repayment outcomes. Monitor higher-risk swap-in and grade F segments closely, especially where the $1,500 floor is driving most approvals. Tighten or diagnose merchants with meaningful application volume but weak completion or weaker repayment outcomes.

Before broad expansion, request fully loaded account economics and a standard-policy counterfactual joined to application number.
